### Description (4000 char max)
```
Brick'd helps you find foods that support healthy testosterone — and shows you the evidence behind every recommendation.

Most nutrition apps hand you a number and ask you to trust it. Brick'd shows its work.

SCAN YOUR GROCERY RECEIPT
Photograph a receipt and Brick'd reads it, recognises the foods, and gives your whole haul a Cart Score. Scan again next week and watch the number move. It tells you which nutrients your cart is short on and exactly what to buy to fix it.

SCAN A BARCODE
Point your camera at any packaged product for an instant score, the nutrients it actually reports, and honest warnings for added sugar, sodium, saturated fat and ultra-processing.

SEARCH 300,000+ FOODS
Real USDA FoodData Central lab measurements — not marketing claims. Results are ranked so the grocery item you meant comes first.

THE BRICK'D SCORE
Each food is scored 0–100 on how much 100g contributes toward the nutrient intakes involved in normal testosterone production. Weights are ranked by the strength of the human evidence:

• Zinc — strong evidence, for correcting deficiency
• Vitamin D — moderate, mainly when deficient
• Protein — moderate
• Magnesium — emerging
• Selenium — limited

Tap any food to see exactly how its points were earned, plus a plain-language summary of the research behind each nutrient.

HONEST BY DESIGN
No food raises testosterone above normal in someone who is already nutrient-sufficient. Brick'd says so, in the app. Points are capped at sufficiency, because megadosing zinc isn't supported by the evidence. Warning flags never secretly subtract from a score — they're shown as facts beside it, so you can judge for yourself.

SHOPPING LIST + NEARBY STORES
Save foods to a shopping list with in-store check-offs, find grocery stores near you with directions, and get culturally relevant staples at ethnic groceries.

NO ACCOUNT REQUIRED
Browse as a guest with no sign-up. Create an account only if you want your saved foods synced across devices. No ads. No data brokers. No tracking.

Brick'd shares nutrition evidence. It is not medical advice. If you have symptoms of low testosterone, the right move is a morning blood test with a doctor — not a shopping list.
```

## Screenshots

Apple requires at least one set for a 6.7" or 6.9" iPhone. Anything you
capture on a modern iPhone Pro Max is the right size; other iPhones work if
you upscale to the exact pixel dimensions.

| Device class | Required pixels |
|---|---|
| 6.9" (15/16 Pro Max) | 1320 × 2868 |
| 6.7" (14/15 Plus, Pro Max) | 1290 × 2796 |

### Shot list (in order — the first two matter most)

1. **Receipt result** — Cart Score ring with the "▲ vs your last haul" badge
   and "Fill your gaps" below.
   *Caption: "Scan your receipt. See your Cart Score."*
2. **Food detail** — score ring + "Why this score" bars.
   *Caption: "Every point, explained."*
3. **Barcode result** — a product with the "Heads up" warning flags visible.
   *Caption: "Honest warnings, not just a number."*
4. **Foods list** — search results or featured picks.
   *Caption: "300,000+ foods, real lab data."*
5. **Stores** — nearby list, or a store detail with your list and gaps.
   *Caption: "Your list, at the store you're standing in."*
6. **How the score works** — the evidence-tier table.
   *Caption: "Ranked by evidence, not marketing."*

### How to capture
1. Open the installed home-screen app on your iPhone (not Safari — you want the
   fullscreen chrome-free view).
2. Set up each state, press **Side + Volume Up** to screenshot.
3. AirDrop them to your PC.
4. If your iPhone isn't a Pro Max, upscale to the exact pixel size above —
   any image editor, or ask Claude to batch-resize them.

Tip: turn on dark mode (Profile → Appearance) — the dark UI screenshots
noticeably better in the App Store's light listing page.
